Reading a standard

STEELS standards are three-dimensional. Each performance expectation weaves together what students know (the core idea, organized here by strand), what they do (the Science and Engineering Practices), and how they think (Crosscutting Concepts for Science and ELS, or Technology and Engineering Practices for T&E).

3 . 1 . 6-8 . A  →  STEELS · Life Science · Grades 6–8 · Standard A

Disciplines: 3.1 Life Science, 3.2 Physical Science, 3.3 Earth & Space Science, 3.4 Environmental Literacy & Sustainability, 3.5 Technology & Engineering. Science standards are grade-specific for K–5 and banded for 6–8 and 9–12. ELS and T&E standards are banded K–2, 3–5, 6–8, and 9–12. Choosing a single grade also shows the band that contains it.

ETS marks the Engineering, Technology, and Applications of Science standards. They sit inside Technology & Engineering, but PDE notes they apply across all STEELS content areas.

Link to any standard by adding its code after a # in this page's address, for example #3.4.6-8.C. Add #glossary to open the glossary.

The PDF button on each standard opens its official Foundation Box from PDE's Standards Aligned System.

About this page

The standards belong to Pennsylvania. Every standard on this page was written and adopted by the Pennsylvania Department of Education (PDE) and the State Board of Education. Their wording is reproduced here unchanged, so teachers can search, filter, and compare them in one place.

The finder was built by Amanda Jeane Strode, a Pennsylvania educator, as a free and independent reference tool. It reorganizes PDE's published documents. It does not add to or change the standards.

Always check the SAS STEELS page for the current official version.

How it was made

Standard text, strands, and codes were transcribed from PDE's K-12 STEELS Standards document. Know, Do, and Think tags were taken from PDE's grade-level Science Curriculum Frameworks and from the Foundation Box for each ELS and T&E standard, then checked against the original documents. Each PDF button links to PDE's own Foundation Box file.

The page was built with the help of Claude, an AI assistant, which extracted and organized the source documents under Amanda Jeane's direction. The glossary definitions were written for this resource, based on PDE's documents, since PDE does not publish a STEELS glossary.
